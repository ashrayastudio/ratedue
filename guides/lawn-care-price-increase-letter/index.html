Lawn-care price-increase letter for existing customers

Before you write a price-increase letter, review what each customer currently pays. An older price might need changing—or it might be a price you deliberately want to keep. A simple review helps you make that decision before sending the same notice to everyone.

RateDue is an iPhone app for reviewing existing client prices against your own current standard. You can start free with one active service and three active client rates. It does not find market prices or send messages to customers automatically.

Review the price before writing the letter

For each customer, note the service, agreed price, date it last changed, and expected number of visits. Compare like with like: the same lawn, service scope and billing unit. A mowing-only price is not directly comparable with a package that includes additional work.

Use the price you currently charge for that service as your standard. The question is how an existing agreement compares with your own current pricing—not what every lawn-care business should charge.

Then choose the next step:

  • Keep the price. Mark it reviewed or intentionally grandfather it.
  • Change the price. Decide on the new amount and when it should take effect.
  • Plan smaller steps. If a single increase does not suit the relationship, consider a gradual plan. Staged plans in RateDue require Pro.

A fictional example

Suppose a customer pays $45 per mowing visit, while your current standard for that same service is $52. You expect 30 visits, with one billable unit per visit.

The estimated difference over those visits is:

($52 − $45) × 30 = $210

That is a comparison of two prices, not promised additional income. Visits may change, and a customer may choose a different service or stop using it. The example is fictional; $52 is not a suggested market rate.

Use your actual expected visit count. Weekly mowing during a season does not necessarily mean 52 visits a year. For an annual estimate in RateDue, use the expected annual frequency and units per visit. When the agreed price meets or exceeds your standard, the below-standard gap is zero.

A letter you can adapt

Subject: An update to your lawn-care service price

Hi [Customer name],

Thank you for choosing [Business name] to look after your lawn.

Starting [effective date], the price for [service and scope] will be [new price] per [visit or billing unit], compared with your current price of [current price].

[Confirm whether the service scope and schedule stay the same, or clearly explain any changes.]

Please contact me at [contact details] if you have any questions about this update.

Thank you,

[Your name]

[Business name]

Replace every placeholder and check the amount, service and date before using the letter. Review your agreement and any applicable notice requirements rather than assuming one notice period fits every customer. Send the message yourself through your usual customer communication channel.

This template is free to copy from the guide. RateDue's optional in-app message templates are a separate Pro feature.

Do you have to raise every customer's price?

No. A review can end with keeping an existing price. The useful step is making that decision deliberately and choosing when to review it again.

Avoid treating the estimated gap as an instruction to charge more. Consider the service, agreement and relationship before deciding what to do.
